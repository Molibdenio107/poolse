# Decisions

Product decisions taken in conversation, one line each, newest last. They live here
because the reasoning otherwise survives only in a chat log nobody reads again — and
several of these reverse an earlier decision, which is exactly the kind of thing that gets
quietly re-reversed six months later by somebody reading the older comment.

Schema decisions live in `docs/data-model.md`; standing conventions live in `CLAUDE.md`.
This file is for the calls themselves.

Most entries are one line. A sizing or pricing call that sets numbers other decisions will
be measured against gets a dated section of its own instead, because the numbers are the
decision and a line that omits them is not usable later.

## 2026-09-06 — Management user sizing per tenant

**Decision.** Poolse targets 10–30 management logins per tenant (Owner, Admin,
Instructor, Maintenance). Nothing may degrade up to ~150. Students and
Encarregados de Educação are excluded from this count and scale independently.

**Rules.**
- Exactly one Owner per tenant, enforced. Transfer ownership is the only way to
  change it.
- Seat cap is a soft quota: `organization.max_management_users` (nullable =
  unlimited), checked when an invitation is *created*, not when accepted.
- Pending, unexpired invitations count toward the quota — the 24h expiry window
  would otherwise let a tenant overshoot.
- Staff list needs search + pagination beyond ~30 rows.

**Plan tiers (indicative, not final).** Starter 5 seats / 1 facility · Pro 25 ·
Business 100 · above that, custom.

**Rationale.** A single municipal pool or swim school runs ~1 owner, 1–2 admins,
6–15 instructors, 2–4 maintenance staff. A multi-facility câmara group reaches
40–60. Every management user is a billed Clerk MAU. The quota exists so the
pricing model is enforceable before the second, paying tenant — the first
production tenant is a free pilot and will not test the limit.

**Open.** Whether students and guardians become Clerk users at all when the
mobile app ships, or get a lighter auth path.

**Not built in this pass.** `max_management_users` is not in the schema yet; it
goes in with the next migration that already touches `organization`.

## Round 6 — Espaços, cleaning and maintenance requests

- **2026-09-06** — A facility's non-pool areas are `space`, not `room`. The concept has to hold the car park and the plant room as comfortably as it holds a balneário, and "room" quietly excludes both.
- **2026-09-06** — `maintenance_request` is built as the seed of Módulo 2 rather than as a spaces-only feature: keyed on a facility, with nullable `space_id`, `pool_id` and `inventory_item_id`. Equipment and tank faults will extend this table instead of arriving with one of their own, because two places to look for "what is broken at this site" is the failure the module exists to prevent.
- **2026-09-06** — Overdue is derived at query time, in SQL, in one place. No `is_overdue` column, no cron job, no worker — a stored flag needs a process to keep it true, and per-tenant running cost is a design constraint. The API ships the boolean and the client renders it rather than re-deriving it.
- **2026-09-06** — An archived cleaning log did not happen: every last-cleaned read filters it out, so deleting a mistaken entry puts the space back to overdue. The alternative leaves a dirty room looking clean because somebody corrected a mistake.
- **2026-09-06** — A space with a cleaning interval and no cleaning at all is overdue, not blank. An absence of history is not evidence of cleanliness, and it is exactly the state the feature exists to surface.
- **2026-09-06** — `space.active = false` means out of service and is exempt from the overdue rule; `archived_at` remains deletion. Two flags earn their place only by meaning different things, and the exemption is what gives `active` a job — nobody cleans a balneário shut for building works.
- **2026-09-06** — Enum values stay English snake_case and the Portuguese is an i18n key. `restock`, not `reposicao`: that word is already the make-up-lesson module's, and one word meaning two unrelated things in one schema is how somebody joins the wrong table at midnight.
- **2026-09-06** — Logging a cleaning and reporting an issue are open to every management login, including instructors; resolving is owner, admin or maintenance. Reporting is noticing, resolving is a judgement that the work was done — and a feature that made an instructor find an admin to record that they mopped the balneário would simply not be used.
- **2026-09-06** — A cleaning log has no edit control at all. An entry is a claim about a moment; correcting one means archiving it, which is owner/admin.
- **2026-09-06** — Resolving an already-resolved issue is a 409, not a silent second write. Two people closing the same fault from two phones must not quietly rewrite who fixed it and when.
- **2026-09-06** — A space's type is optional and defaults to `other`, matching the column default; only a *stated* invalid type is refused. A club naming a room the six categories do not cover must not be stopped by the classification.
- **2026-09-06** — The free-text inventory locations became spaces, matched case- and accent-insensitively per facility, named with the spelling on the earliest-created item. Only non-archived items mint a space; archived ones are linked where one already exists. `inventory_item.location` stays in place — dropping it is a separate change so the result can be eyeballed first.
- **2026-09-06** — The API answers `canManage`, `canLog` and `canResolve` on the read, from the same helper the guards use. The screen shows a control only where pressing it would work, and the client never derives a permission from a role list.
- **2026-09-06** — Espaços appears on both Instalações (inside each site's card, under that site's tanks) and the site's own page, from one component with a `variant`. Asked for on the list screen first and then on both; a second copy of the panel is how two screens start disagreeing about what a space is.
- **2026-09-06** — Instalações fetches one space list per site in parallel rather than folding them into `/facilities`. A loop over an endpoint is usually wrong; a licence bounds a club to one or two sites, and the alternative puts every site's cleaning state behind the request that draws the page and loses the property that a failed block costs only itself.

## Round 6 — the calendar's week grid

- **2026-09-06** — The calendar gets its own grid; the Turmas screen keeps `schedule-board.tsx`. They were one 4,166-line component told apart by whether a `weekStart` was passed, so every change to the dated view was a change to the recurring one. Two grids overlap for now; folding Turmas onto the new one is proposed, not done.
- **2026-09-06** — The axis stays a week: seven days, each subdivided by pista, with time down the left on a pixel scale. A single-day view with lanes as columns was the other candidate and would have been closer to Google Calendar; keeping the week is what the screen is for.
- **2026-09-06** — Blocks are placed from their minutes at one pixel per minute, replacing slot-as-table-row. A 45-minute class in a 60-minute slot used to fill the row and read as an hour, which is a lie the operator had to know to discount.
- **2026-09-06** — A drop asks *this week / every week* in a popover at the cursor rather than a centred modal, and the block has already moved by the time it asks. Round 5's decision that the board must ask rather than guess stands; only the staging changed.
- **2026-09-06** — Snapping is to the facility's own slot rows, not to a fixed increment, falling back to the greatest common divisor of the grid's own times where no row covers the moment. There is no stored increment, and a real timetable is a list of rows rather than a number.
- **2026-09-06** — One droppable per day-and-lane column instead of one per cell. Measured on the reference club: 2,256 droppables became 168, a 13.4× cut, and that count — each cell running a rules evaluation on drag start — was the clunkiness, not the drag library.
- **2026-09-06** — Event colour is the level, in the club's own level order, over eight solid tints. Washed tints at 15% measured ΔE 4.5 apart, close enough that the colour carried no information; solid ones are ΔE 26.7 apart with white text at 4.6:1 or better in both themes.
- **2026-09-06** — A parceria keeps its partner's colour, and a turma with no level takes the neutral rather than the first tint. "Not said" must not look like "Iniciados".
- **2026-09-06** — The hover card is removed from the tree during a drag rather than given `pointer-events: none`. The ticket asked for the latter; it would have disabled Take the register and Cancel class, which are buttons inside the card that round 6 deliberately moved there.
- **2026-09-06** — Clicking an empty cell places an existing unscheduled turma rather than opening the new-turma form. That form has no day or time fields on purpose — a turma with no days is a valid half-finished thing — so the day and time a click carries would have gone nowhere. The lane is still not set by the click, because `placeSlotAction` returns no schedule id; dragging the block one column is the answer until it does.
- **2026-09-06** — The calendar gets a wider content column (`max-w-page-wide`, 104rem) and is the only page allowed one. AC5's rejection of per-page widths holds for pages made of text; it was never about a screen whose content is a grid, where the cap protects nobody and costs half the monitor.
- **2026-09-06** — The calendar draws only the weekdays the site opens on, from the facility's opening hours — keeping round 5's exception that a closed day still carrying a class is drawn anyway, shaded. A class that is invisible is a class nobody can move.
- **2026-09-06** — Classes on a holiday, on a day the pool is shut, or on a day already past are all faded, and **all three stay draggable**. This reverses the round-5 rule that past classes are undraggable: in use it read as a broken grid, since the block looks like every other block and does not move. Rearranging last Monday from Wednesday is ordinary planning, and the server already refuses what is genuinely impossible.
- **2026-09-06** — The lane header names the row "Pistas" once and numbers the columns by each lane's position in its pool, rather than repeating the lane's name on every column. Position rather than digits parsed out of the name: a club with "Raia A" and "Central" has no digits to parse, and a mix would put a column headed 4 in third place. The name stays on hover and in the hover card.
- **2026-09-06** — The calendar card no longer scrolls vertically: it is as tall as the day and the page scrolls. The day and lane headers scroll away as a result, which was accepted knowingly — `overflow-x: auto` forces `overflow-y` to auto, so a container that scrolls sideways cannot also stick to the viewport vertically. The time gutter keeps its sideways stickiness, which is the axis that still scrolls.
- **2026-09-06** — A block's left and right edges resize its lane span. The change applies to every week and the confirmation offers one button saying so, rather than a two-way choice whose answers would both do the same thing: lanes live on the recurring booking and `moveOccurrenceAction` carries only a start time.
- **2026-09-06** — Lane columns narrowed again, 92px to 64px. A six-lane pool now fits a week across a laptop without sideways scrolling, which is worth more than the tail of a truncated turma name — the full name is on the hover card.

## Round 6 — colour, and the calendar's second pass

- **2026-09-06** — A turma can carry its own colour, stored as a `class_colour` enum of eight token names rather than a hex. The same three reasons `booking_category.colour` gave: colour comes from tokens, a hex is one colour for two themes, and each of the eight was measured for contrast. A free picker lets a club take its own block text to 1.3:1 with no warning. `partner.colour` stays a hex; it predates this and a slice about turmas is not where that gets changed.
- **2026-09-06** — A turma with no colour falls back to its level's tint rather than going neutral, so nothing changes for a club until somebody picks something.
- **2026-09-06** — The calendar's hover card closes after 220ms, not immediately. "Closes immediately on pointer-out" was taken literally from the ticket and made the card useless: Marcar presenças and Cancelar aula are inside it, and the pointer has to cross the gap to reach them. The delay is travel time.
- **2026-09-06** — Take the register and Cancel class also appear at the foot of the lesson-plan sheet. Clicking a class is how most people arrive; being told to close it and hover the block instead is how a feature goes unused.
- **2026-09-06** — The move/resize confirmation is anchored to the block it changed, with a tail pointing at it, and flips above the block when it would otherwise open off the bottom of the window.
- **2026-09-06** — The Espaços section starts collapsed on both screens, with counts of spaces, overdue cleanings and open issues in its header. A shut section is only safe if what it is hiding is still legible.
- **2026-09-06** — An open issue on a space is marked in amber with its own icon, distinct from the red of overdue cleaning. A spotless space with a broken shower is not the same problem.
- **2026-09-06** — `SpacesPanel` takes where "back" should go, instead of assuming the site's page. Opening a space from Instalações and pressing Voltar used to land somewhere the operator had never been.
- **2026-09-06** — `classes.colours.magenta` is listed in `SAME_ON_PURPOSE`: it is the same word in both languages. Renaming the pt-PT one to "fúcsia" to satisfy the guard would be choosing a different colour to dodge a check.

## Round 6 — colours, stand-ins and leave

- **2026-09-06** — A level carries its own colour, backfilled from the position the calendar was deriving it from. Nothing changed visually on the day, and the derived rule was deleted rather than left as a fallback nobody would remember. `class_colour` is reused rather than duplicated: a level's green and a turma's green must be the same green.
- **2026-09-06** — On the Classes screen a turma's left rule is its own colour, falling back to its level's, so a turma looks the same there as on the calendar without anybody colouring anything.
- **2026-09-06** — Changing the teacher on the calendar is a stand-in for that lesson only, written to `class_session.substitute_instructor_membership_id`. Reassigning the turma stays on the turma's own screen. Next week goes back to normal by itself, which is what a stand-in means.
- **2026-09-06** — An instructor on approved leave is shown in the stand-in list, greyed, with the reason, and cannot be chosen; the endpoint refuses them again. Hiding them would answer "where is Ana?" with silence, and "on holiday" and "left the club" are different things.
- **2026-09-06** — Availability is read from approved leave only. A request nobody has answered is not an absence, and treating it as one would let an unanswered form veto the timetable.
- **2026-09-06** — Medical and personal leave join `vacation_request` as a `kind` rather than getting a table of their own — one approval queue, one thing for the calendar to ask. Only `vacation` counts against the yearly entitlement: a week of flu is not a week of holiday.
- **2026-09-06** — The staff list shows who is away *today*, from approved leave, with the kind in words. Only today, because that list answers "who is here" rather than "who has anything booked this year".
- **2026-09-06** — The calendar's day boundaries are a 2px `border-strong` rule against the lanes' hairlines. At 64px columns the week read as one undifferentiated ladder and finding Thursday meant counting.
- **2026-09-06** — The first hour label sits just below its line rather than centred on it. Centred put half of it above the canvas, where the header covered it — and the club's opening hour is the time somebody is most likely to be looking for.
- **2026-09-06** — The lesson-plan sheet is `max-w-2xl` rather than the dialog's usual `max-w-md`. A plan is prose being composed, and at 28rem a line of sets wrapped twice.
- **2026-09-06** — The slot-grid builder is removed from the facility's Opening hours card; the slot rows stay and keep driving the calendar's snapping, the Turmas board, the printed sheet and the XLSX export. What the calendar made pointless was arranging those rows by hand, not the rows.
- **2026-09-06** — The calendar's legend became one card that is also the filter: a checkbox, a swatch and a count per level, plus parcerias, eventos and manutenção, laid out in columns like the vacations team map. A legend that tells you what a colour means without letting you act on it is a longer route to the same place.
- **2026-09-06** — A dragged block shows the time it would land at rather than the time it came from. The block is under the pointer and the gutter is a week away on the left, so reading the landing time off the ruler means looking away from the thing being moved.
- **2026-09-06** — Today's column keeps a faint tint and pulses once for two seconds when the week is opened, dropped under `prefers-reduced-motion`. A column that goes on blinking cannot be read.
- **2026-09-06** — `DAY_RULE` ties the block layer's arithmetic to the day separator's width. They disagreed once — the rule went to 2px and the maths kept adding 1 — which drifted every block up to seven pixels by Sunday and made a drag near a lane edge land one lane over from where it looked.
- **2026-09-06** — A refused move shows the server's own sentence alongside the generic key. Refusals come from several different rules and folding them into "it did not work" produces reports nobody can act on.
- **2026-09-07** — A one-week move carries its pistas, reversing the 2026-09-06 decision that a lane change applies to every week. That decision was honest about a limitation rather than a preference: `moveOccurrenceAction` carried a start time and nothing else, so "só esta semana" could not express a lane. A session has its own rows in `class_session_lane`, so it can — and "pista 3 is shut for repairs this Tuesday" is a real thing a club needs to say. The scope popover therefore offers both answers for a sideways change as it does for a move in time.
- **2026-09-07** — The tank selector moves to the left of the calendar toolbar and gets a primary border. It decides which pistas the whole week is drawn from, and it was sitting on the right at the same visual weight as "Ver todos" — the page's most consequential control dressed as a tidy-up button.
- **2026-09-07** — The seed's leave fixtures gain a second kind: somebody away *today*, anchored to the facility's own date rather than a fixed one. The three demo states are a week next April, which is right for the vacation map and invisible on the staff list — so the away chip could only ever be checked by editing the database by hand.
- **2026-09-07** — A class whose register has been taken cannot be moved, in either scope. One mark means somebody stood at the poolside with a list, so the day, hour and pista are a record rather than a plan. This does *not* re-open round 6's reversal about past classes: the block stays draggable, because one that looks like every other block and silently refuses reads as a broken grid. The server refuses and says why, and the hover card shows "Presenças: já marcadas" beforehand.
- **2026-09-07** — The calendar's horizontal scale moves to `lib/calendar-scale.ts` beside the vertical one, and a drag resolves its lane from travel rather than from the column under the pointer. Keeping one axis in the component and one in the library is what let them be answered by different questions: the time from how far the pointer had moved, the lane from what it was over. ## Round 11 — the four questions invoicing could not be started without

- **2026-09-08** — **These are internal records, shaped so certification is additive.** Asked before any code, because it decides everything under it. Decreto-Lei 28/2019 wants certified software, a validation code obtained from the AT per series, an ATCUD and a QR on every document and a SAF-T (PT) export — a programme with an external dependency, not an evening. So Poolse produces a numbered, immutable record a club hands to whatever issues its faturas, and the screens say so in both languages rather than leaving a club to hear it from its accountant. Two columns are reserved and null — `invoice_series.at_validation_code`, `invoice.atcud` — and they are the only speculative thing in the module. What is *not* speculative is the shape, because the shape is what cannot be retrofitted onto documents a family has already been sent.
- **2026-09-08** — **A numbering series belongs to a facility.** A club running two pools numbers each site's documents in its own book, and the answer is not recoverable later without renumbering. The licence already bounds how many sites a subscription has, so for almost every tenant this is one book hanging off the one facility — and the club that grows into two does not have to renumber. The *prefix*, though, is unique per **organization**: the club is one legal entity and two sites both numbering `FT A/1` would issue two different documents under one number, so a second site takes the first free suffix and can be renamed while its book is empty.
- **2026-09-08** — **A monthly run and a per-student action, and they are one call.** The second is the first with `studentIds` set. Two entry points would be two queries and eventually two answers about what a month owes; a club with 200 students will not click 200 times, and a joiner on the 12th cannot wait for next month.
- **2026-09-08** — **One document per payer, so siblings land together.** The payer is the primary guardian, or the student themselves where there is no guardian — the adult path, and also a student nobody has finished registering. A family paying one amount by débito direto in 2.3 needs one document to collect against, and the payer relationship is the expensive half to retrofit onto numbers already issued. Lines keep their `student_id`, so a per-student view is a filter rather than a different document.

## Round 11 — the numbering, and the four ways it is kept honest

- **2026-09-08** — **`next_number` is a column, not a Postgres sequence, and the difference is the whole module.** A sequence is not transactional: a rolled-back insert consumes its number and leaves a gap, which is the one thing a numbering series may not do. The `UPDATE … RETURNING` that reads and bumps the column rolls back with its transaction. `invoices.sql` test 3 asserts exactly that, and it is the test to keep if the file is ever cut down — a well-meaning refactor to a sequence passes every other assertion in it.
- **2026-09-08** — **The allocation lives in a BEFORE INSERT trigger on `invoice`, and an INSERT that supplies a number is refused.** The prompt asked for numbering that is structurally safe rather than carefully written, and this is what that means: the allocation and the row it numbers are one statement, so application code cannot hold a number it did not use, cannot retry and skip one, and cannot be written tired in a way that does either.
- **2026-09-08** — **The row lock that allocation takes is load-bearing beyond numbering.** It serialises every insert into one series, which is what makes the double-billing constraint trigger sound under two people pressing the button at once. Written down because it looks like a side effect and is in fact the reason a constraint trigger was allowed at all.
- **2026-09-08** — **A document is written once, said with a privilege rather than a trigger.** `poolse_app` holds SELECT and INSERT on `invoice` and `invoice_line` and nothing else — a revoke, since `core-tenancy` grants all four by default. A trigger would do the same job and could be dropped by a later migration that meant something else; a missing privilege cannot be forgotten by application code at all. Same family as `audit_log` and `consent`, which the harness already cleans up over the owner's connection for the same reason. There is therefore no `updated_at` and no `archived_at` on either table: a column nothing can ever change is a lie. Settlement, in 2.3, arrives as a child table exactly as `student_fee_payment` did — not as a column on a document.

## Round 11 — one charge per occurrence, and the half everybody forgets

- **2026-09-08** — **"Billed twice" is refused by a constraint trigger, not a partial unique index.** The index is the obvious instrument and it is wrong: after a credit note the occurrence is chargeable again, and that is the ordinary way a club fixes a document it got wrong. "Live" means "not since credited", which needs a join a partial index cannot do. The test that matters is the pair — the second charge refused, and the same charge allowed once the credit note exists.
- **2026-09-08** — **`invoice_charged_on` is one definition with two readers**: the trigger that refuses a second charge and the monthly run that must not offer one. Written twice they agree until somebody fixes a bug in one of them, and a preview offering what the commit then refuses is a failure the operator cannot see the cause of. It takes an `except_line_id` so the AFTER-INSERT trigger can ask about a row already inserted; excluding by line rather than by document also catches one occurrence written twice onto one document.
- **2026-09-08** — **A credit note is whole-document, not per line.** Partial credits are expressible later without breaking anything — each credit line already names the invoice line it reverses — and whole-document is what a small club actually does. One credit note per document, because a document credited twice makes "has this been corrected" have two answers and doubles anything summed from the pair.
- **2026-09-08** — **A credit note is numbered in its own book.** Sharing one series between faturas and credit notes would make each type's sequence gappy, which is the thing the series exists to prevent. The kind travels into the composite key `(organization_id, series_id, kind)`, so a fatura cannot be numbered in the credit note book — a key rather than a trigger, because a key cannot be raced.

## Round 11 — what a line may say, and what it may not

- **2026-09-08** — **The line stores the club's own words and never the translated kind.** Discovered by the tests rather than by reading: `fee_plan` has no `name` column at all — a plan's label is its kind, its level and its frequency, composed on the client. So `description` holds the level's or the season's name, `lessons_per_week` holds the frequency, and `kind` stays an enum whose Portuguese is an i18n key. A document storing "Mensalidade" would read half in Portuguese for a club working in English, and would be frozen at the language of whoever pressed the button. `description` is nullable because a plain quota has no such word, and a NOT NULL would have forced one to be invented.
- **2026-09-08** — **`invoice_line.student_tax_number` is not a duplicate of the payer's.** `student.tax_number` has existed since round 8 precisely because a parent deducting lessons on their IRS does it against the *child's* number — so a document addressed to a guardian routinely carries a different number per line, which one column on the document could not express for a family with two children. (A question was asked about where an adult's own NIF lives; the column already existed, with a better argument than the one proposed. No gap.)
- **2026-09-08** — **Totals are summed in SQL and not stored.** The lines cannot change, so a stored total could not drift either — but this way there is one definition rather than two, and `invoice_vat_cents` is the same function the preview calls, so a run and the document it became show the same tax.
- **2026-09-08** — **A run's occurrence is derived from the line's own start walked forward by its periodicity**, not from `current_period_start`. That function answers "what is this line being asked for *today*", and a club invoicing March in April needs March. A line charged once has exactly one occurrence, on its `starts_on`, and every join to `fee_period` is a LEFT JOIN — an inner one drops every inscrição and every seguro from the run, which is the failure the register's "pago" tick already found once.
- **2026-09-08** — **The count of occurrences left out is reported, not dropped.** "There is nothing to bill" and "everything here is already billed" look identical on an empty table and are different things for an operator to do. Silence is what makes a run read as a feature that did nothing — the same lesson as round 7's `weeksKept`.

## Round 12 — settlement, and the state nobody stores

- **2026-09-08** — **A payment is a child row, never a column on the document**, as 2.2 promised. `invoice` holds no UPDATE grant for the app role, so a `settled_on` column could not have been written even by somebody who wanted one — but the honest reason is that a family paying half in October and half in November is two facts, and a single date holds neither. `payment_source` is reused from the fee register rather than reinvented: a second enum meaning the same thing in different words is how two screens end up disagreeing about what `sepa` is.
- **2026-09-08** — **There is no status column, and `invoice-settlement.sql` test 2 asserts the schema has not grown one.** Every input is already stored — `total − paid`, `due_on` against today, whether a credit note exists — and a fifth fact restating the other four would need a worker to keep it true, which is a per-tenant cost. `invoice_status` is that derivation, in SQL, STABLE because it reads `current_date`. The same argument as the overdue-cleaning rule in `spaces.repository.ts`.
- **2026-09-08** — **The precedence is credited, then paid, then overdue.** A partly paid document past its due date is still overdue — half of nothing arriving on time is still late — and a credited document is owed by nobody even where money had already arrived: the payment stays on the record, the debt does not. Both are the kind of rule somebody "simplifies" by reading the CASE from the bottom, so both are asserted.
- **2026-09-08** — **`invoice_dates_ordered` is dropped, and nothing replaces it.** 2.2 wrote `CHECK (due_on >= issued_on)` on the assumption that a document is issued before it falls due. That is wrong for the ordinary case 2.3 exists to serve: bill October on the 2nd of December and the run dates the document today with a due date six weeks behind, and the constraint refused it as a bare `23514` naming no field. There is no ordering rule between those two dates that is true of every real document, and a constraint that is right most of the time fails on the case somebody is actually in.
- **2026-09-08** — Found by the first integration test that tried to produce an overdue document, which is the only way to produce one. Worth recording because it is the second time this module's tests have caught something `tsc` could not see — the first was `fee_plan` having no `name` column at all.
- **2026-09-08** — **An overpayment settles rather than owing the family money.** `outstandingCents` floors at zero: a negative figure on a chase list is a number somebody would try to collect. A rounding cent over is not a case worth modelling as a refund.
- **2026-09-08** — **A payment is soft-deleted.** An entry against the wrong document has to be removable, and a hard DELETE would take the record of the mistake with it. Every sum filters `archived_at`, and the test asserts the archived row is still there.
- **2026-09-08** — **A credit note is never paid, refused by a trigger rather than by a screen.** There is a second way in as soon as anything reconciles a bank feed, and a rule that lives in one form is a rule the other path does not have.

## Round 12 — chasing, without pretending to send anything

A season is what turmas live in and there are none; it is not created rather than created and hidden, so "does this tenant have a season" has one honest answer. The pool is `outdoor` by default — a garden pool usually is, and it is one select on the pool's page if not.
- **2026-09-11** — **Nothing enforces a personal tenant's size.** `max_facilities` already caps the sites at one; a second pool or a second member — a jacuzzi, a partner who also tops up the chlorine — is the person's own affair. The club-only sections are absent from the navigation as a shape, not hidden as a permission: the API answers, and a personal owner typing `/dashboard/classes` harms nobody but their own afternoon.
- **2026-09-11** — **The personal dashboard judges the latest reading; it does not record one.** The tiles say in range, out of range with the bound crossed, or "no range set" — three states, because a metric the pool does not judge must not look like one that happens to be fine. Recording stays on the pool's page, for the reason the tasks panel gives.
- **2026-09-11** — **`energy_reading` is hypertable-shaped and not a hypertable.** The natural key `(organization_id, meter_id, taken_at)` with no surrogate id is the one thing Timescale requires; the conversion is one `create_hypertable` when automated feeds arrive. Not converted now because a club types one figure a month, and enabling the extension is the hosting constraint the phase-0 note names as the half that cannot be swapped — doing that for a few hundred rows a year buys nothing for the free pilot. The stack choice stands; its trigger is the deferred item "automated meter feeds".
- **2026-09-11** — **`reads` is set once and never edited.** Changing what a value means under a year of readings would make every one of them wrong at once. A meter set up wrong is retired and made again; a replaced dial is a new meter with `replaced_meter_id`, and the old one is retired in the same transaction so its name is free.
- **2026-09-11** — **Consumption is attributed to the reading that closes the interval.** A reading on the 15th spans two months and the database cannot know the split; the chart's caption says "the consumption the readings of that month established". An empty month is null and drawn as a labelled gap, never a zero-height bar — "nobody read the meter" and "nothing was used" are different facts.
- **2026-09-11** — **A dial that runs backwards is refused with the neighbouring figure.** Same contract as `pool_capacity`: the trigger's DETAIL carries the number, the API sends it as structure, the form quotes it. An archived reading leaves the series so a typo is corrected by remove-and-retype on the same instant; a *live* reading at the same instant is a 409, not an overwrite.
- **2026-09-11** — **5.1 and 5.2 shipped as one slice.** A schema with no entry form is not testable, and the roadmap's "a month of data is visible" is what a tester can actually check.
- **2026-09-11** — **Energia is a top-level section, and the panel on the site page stays.** Rui's call: logging the month's readings is one job across every site, and starting from Instalações puts a site page between the operator and the dial. One screen lists every site with its meters — a one-site club lands straight on them. Visible to both kinds of tenant, since a garden pool has a pump.
- **2026-09-11** — **Energia is owner, admin and maintenance, whole.** Rui's call, narrower than planned maintenance: what running the site costs is not an instructor's question. The menu, the site-page panel and every route agree; the API refuses the rest. Placed directly under Instalações, because it is about the building rather than the people in it.
- **2026-09-11** — **A bill is three tables, read off two real EDP documents, not the "summary columns" I first proposed.** One PDF bundles several faturas (only one is electricity); the dial has registers even on a Simples tariff; billed lines are split by VAT rate and carry discounts. Rui chose the faithful shape over the flatter one. The subtotal is every line before VAT *including* DGEG and IEC — the paper's "5,11 €" block is taxes and VAT together, and splitting it is what makes the lines add up.
- **2026-09-11** — **The import fills the manual form; the form is what gets sent.** Two ways in, one pipeline, one preview: a bill read off a PDF and a bill typed by hand walk the same `draftToBody` and the same API check. Numbers stay as printed ("15,41 €") until that one function.
- **2026-09-11** — **The parser's prompt describes concepts, never a layout.** EDP, Galp and Iberdrola print the same facts in different places; a prompt that knew where EDP puts the CPE would be wrong for the next supplier. The strict tool schema is the contract; the preview is where a person catches what a new layout confused.
- **2026-09-11** — **Real bills are never committed.** The two EDP PDFs carry names, NIFs, CPEs and meter serials; they live gitignored under `test-fixtures/energy/private/` and the repo's fixture is an invented identity with the same structure. The parser sends the document as it is, holder's name included — the flag is off by default and the form says so.
- **2026-09-11** — **Arithmetic disagreements are warnings; mistyped totals are refusals.** Lines that do not sum to the subtotal, registers that do not match the billed kWh, a foreign CPE, an overlapping period — a person decides. `total ≠ subtotal + VAT` and `document ≠ total + other` are CHECKs, because they mean a figure was mistyped rather than rounded.
- **2026-09-11** — **A bill for a meter with no CPE stamps the meter.** Said on the preview (`willSetCpe`), never silently, and never overwriting a CPE the meter already has — that case is a warning naming both.
- **2026-09-11** — **The Energia screen opens with the drop zone, and a bill with no meter makes one.** Rui's call, twice over: the import was first behind a button, then on a separate page gated on a meter already existing; neither is where an operator holding a stack of bills starts. Now a PDF dropped anywhere on Energia is read, the CPE picks the meter across every site, and a CPE nobody carries proposes creating a "Geral" dial at the site — on filing only, never on preview. The preview is skipped for a not-yet-existing meter and the form says so; the commit still runs every check.
- **2026-09-11** — **Energy cost on the dashboard is the bills, by the month a period ended in.** Same attribution rule as a reading's consumption. VAT included, because that is what left the account; the envelope's other charges are not energy and stay out.

- **2026-09-11** — **Dropping a file onto a page is the Energia screen's gesture, and only there.** The parcerias panel on the site page listened for a drop anywhere on the window, so a bill dragged towards the energy card was refused as "not a spreadsheet". That listener is gone — parcerias import through the button and its file chooser — and the bill form listens for a window drop only when the Energia page asks it to (`dropAnywhere`). A meter's own page keeps the chooser. The pool page's water import and the register, inventory and calendar importers keep theirs; they are not on the site's page.

## 2026-09-12 — Platform administration, slice 1

**Platform admin is not a tenant role; it is orthogonal to Owner/Admin and enforced by
`PlatformAdminGuard` and a dedicated RLS-bypass DB role used only by `PlatformModule`.**

- **2026-09-12** — **Built inside the existing apps.** A route group in the Next.js frontend and a `PlatformModule` in NestJS. No new project, no new deploy, no second Clerk application.
- **2026-09-12** — **Operators are rows in `platform_admin`, keyed on the Clerk user id, seeded by a documented one-off script** (`pnpm db:platform-admin grant|revoke|list`). Not a migration, because a Clerk user id differs between the development, staging and production instances and a literal in a migration would be wrong in two of them. No UI to manage the list in slice 1: there are two or three of these rows for the life of the company, and granting one is meant to need the owner database credentials.
- **2026-09-12** — **Enforcement lives in the backend.** `PlatformAdminGuard` on every controller in `PlatformModule`, with no opt-out decorator. The frontend hides `/admin` from every navigation and redirects a non-admin to `/dashboard`; that is not the control and does not pretend to be.
- **2026-09-12** — **`/platform/(.*)` is authenticated with no tenant** (`IDENTITY_ONLY_ROUTES`). An operator who belongs to no organization must be able to open the area, and `TenantMiddleware` would refuse them with `no_organization` before the guard ever saw them. Three outcomes, told apart by a stable code: `not_platform_admin` (403), `platform_not_configured` (503), or 200.
- **2026-09-12** — **The cross-tenant role is narrow, not `BYPASSRLS`** — the one place the ticket's wording was implemented differently, and Rui's call on the alternatives. `poolse_platform` is a third login on its own pool (`DATABASE_PLATFORM_URL`), named in a `for select to poolse_platform using (true)` policy on seven tables and holding no privilege on the rest of the schema. Granting `BYPASSRLS` needs superuser at migrate time, which the owner role on a managed Postgres is not guaranteed to be; and it would hand the platform `student_sensitive`, `invoice` and everything else for ever. Reach is an allowlist, so widening it is a reviewed line of SQL rather than a consequence of existing. The tenant-facing connection is unchanged and the isolation suite is green.
- **2026-09-12** — **`assertPlatformRoleIsNarrow()` at API boot**, the mirror of `assertRlsApplies()`. Pointing `DATABASE_PLATFORM_URL` at the owner because "both can see everything" would work perfectly and silently give `/admin` write access to the whole schema.
- **2026-09-12** — **`comped` was added to `subscription_status`; `trialing` and `canceled` keep their spellings.** The ticket asked for `trial | active | past_due | cancelled | comped` and the column already existed as `trialing | active | past_due | canceled`. `comped` is the genuinely new value — the free pilot is live and deliberately not billed, and as `active` it would look like revenue. Renaming the other two would mean rewriting three `SECURITY DEFINER` provisioning functions that insert the literal, for no behaviour; Rui chose to add only `comped`.
- **2026-09-12** — **`organization.max_management_users` lands here, reported and not enforced.** Decided 2026-09-06 and recorded as "not in the schema yet"; the overview is what needed it. The refusal at invitation time belongs with the ticket that can also tell an operator how to buy another seat. Null is unlimited, never zero — the same reading as every other ceiling in the schema.
- **2026-09-12** — **Seats count active management memberships plus live invitations.** An invitation already creates its membership row at status `invited`, so counting `active` on one side and not-accepted/not-revoked/not-expired invitations on the other counts each promised seat exactly once. The expiry test is what stops the 24-hour window letting a tenant overshoot by inviting, waiting a day and inviting again.
- **2026-09-12** — **"Last activity" is `max(audit_log.created_at)`** — the cheapest correct definition available. That table already carries `(organization_id, created_at DESC)` and is the only one that moves on every mutation path. It is honestly labelled as the last *recorded write*, not the last login; per-request health has its own retention policy and is a different thing.
- **2026-09-12** — **Every platform request is audited, reads included.** One line in `platform_audit_log` per request, written by an interceptor on the module; the request (search term, page), never the response. A 403 is written by the guard itself, because a guard runs before every interceptor and that is exactly the request worth a record. The table is append-only even to the operator, and `poolse_app` is refused it outright. Reads are logged because the habit has to exist before the actions do — a trail that begins the day somebody can suspend a tenant begins one day too late.
- **2026-09-12** — **`/admin` lives under the `(app)` route group rather than its own.** The URL is identical — a route group contributes no path segment — and a fourth root layout would duplicate `ClerkProvider`, `NextIntlClientProvider`, the theme cookie and the toast stack for no difference the operator can see. It deliberately does not inherit `dashboard/layout.tsx`, which is where the tenant navigation lives; its own bar is inverted so the operator can tell which side they are on from the corner of their eye.
- **2026-09-12** — **The non-admin redirect happens in the page, not the layout.** The page already asks the API a question only an operator may ask, so "may this person be here" and "did that request work" are the same answer: one round trip and one audit line instead of two that could disagree.
- **2026-09-12** — **Read-only on purpose.** Extending a trial, changing a plan, suspending, per-tenant feature flags, "view as", the Clerk MAU pull and cross-tenant analytics are all out. Half a set of actions is a screen whose disabled controls need explaining.

## 2026-09-12 — Platform admin, slice 2: observability

- **2026-09-12** — **Server health is global, not per tenant.** One `/health` checking Postgres, TimescaleDB and Clerk, each with a latency. No CPU or memory: Railway and Fly already provide those, and a second copy of a number somebody else owns is a second copy to disbelieve.
- **2026-09-12** — **Only Postgres can make `/health` answer `down`.** The status code is the deploy gate and nothing else about the endpoint may move it. A product whose release rolls back because its sign-in provider had a bad minute is a product that rolls back during every one of its provider's bad minutes — so Clerk failing is `degraded` and HTTP 200.
- **2026-09-12** — **`not_installed` is its own check status, neither degraded nor down.** TimescaleDB is deliberately absent and Clerk may be stubbed out in development. A red dot for a considered absence is how an operator learns to stop reading the strip.
- **2026-09-12** — **Not built with `@nestjs/terminus`.** The ticket named it; three checks that are a query, a query and a `fetch` did not need a module, a set of indicator classes and a dependency to express, and the `not_installed` state and the Postgres-only-is-fatal rule both sit outside what its default `HealthCheckService` composes. One controller, one config object, and unit tests for the composition.
- **2026-09-12** — **`tenant_request_stats` is hypertable-shaped and conditionally a hypertable** — Rui's call, given that the development image is `postgres:16-alpine` and the hosting question from 2026-09-11 is still open. The natural key `(organization_id, bucket)` with no surrogate id is the half that cannot be retrofitted and is there from the start; the migration's guarded `DO` block converts the table and adds the 30-day retention policy where the extension exists. Where it does not, the API's own minute flush prunes rows past 30 days, at most hourly, on a connection it was opening anyway — so retention holds on both and there is still no scheduled worker and no per-tenant running cost.
- **2026-09-12** — **Never one row per request.** A club at a hundred requests a minute would write 144,000 rows a day, more storage than everything the club actually does, for data whose purpose is a coloured dot. The interceptor aggregates in memory, flushes every 60s and on shutdown, and costs a request a `Map` mutation.
- **2026-09-12** — **A request with no tenant context records nothing**, by construction rather than by a list. Every such route runs outside `tenantStorage`, so the landing page, `/health`, the webhook, the identity-only routes and the whole platform area are skipped without anybody maintaining an exclusion.
- **2026-09-12** — **p95 is merged by taking the larger of the two, and is documented as an approximation.** A true hourly p95 needs every sample kept for the hour, which is the per-request storage the design exists to avoid. It errs high, which is the safe direction for a health signal.
- **2026-09-12** — **`last_request_at` sits beside `last_error_at`.** "The most recent request was an error" is then a comparison rather than an inference from aggregates — which would be wrong on every tenant whose next request succeeded.
- **2026-09-12** — **The tenant role writes the stats and the platform role reads them.** Asymmetric on purpose: the interceptor runs in the request path and must not borrow the platform login, and no screen in the tenant app has any business showing a club its own error rate. `USING` admits the tenant's own row because the upsert's `ON CONFLICT` has to find it; an unscoped read still returns nothing.
- **2026-09-12** — **Health is derived at read time in one place, and `unknown` is not `green`.** Green / amber / red / unknown against a 24-hour window and a 2% rate, both in one config constant, with the newest-request rule catching an outage in its first minute — one 5xx in a thousand is 0.1% and green and on fire. A tenant nobody used is a different fact from one that worked perfectly. 4xx never colours anything: a client meeting a 403 is the API working.
- **2026-09-12** — **The charts are hand-rolled HTML and CSS** — Rui's call. There is no charting library installed; `trend-chart.tsx` and `consumption-bars.tsx` are inline SVG and CSS bars with comments saying a charting bundle is a phase-5 cost. Recharts on an internal ops page one person opens would be that cost bought early. An hour with no requests is a gap, not a zero-height bar, for the same reason an empty month is on the energy chart.
- **2026-09-12** — **The error list is one line per route, not per row.** A route failing every minute for an hour writes itself into sixty buckets; sixty identical lines would describe one problem while hiding every other.
- **2026-09-12** — **The 24h counts are visible text beside the health chip, not only in its tooltip.** The standing rule: a tooltip explains what something means and never carries a fact that appears nowhere else.
- **2026-09-12** — **The tenants table is sorted by health in the page, not in the query.** The verdict is derived at read time and has no column to `ORDER BY`; sorting it in SQL would put the derivation in two places. It sorts the current page rather than the whole list, which is what a reader scanning one screen wants and is said out loud rather than implied.
- **2026-09-12** — **Sentry is wired and off.** Both SDKs no-op without a DSN, so `pnpm dev`, the tests and a fresh clone behave identically. `tenant_id` is tagged at the one moment each side learns the organization — the id, never the name, because a Sentry issue titled with a club's name is the club's data in a third party. `sendDefaultPii` is off and tracing is `0`: PII would undo the 500-character ceiling by default, and tracing is the expensive half of a service whose cost is a design constraint.
- **2026-09-12** — **Capture happens in `BadInputFilter`, not `SentryGlobalFilter`.** That filter is `@Catch()` and terminates the response, so registering Sentry's alongside it means one of the two never runs — either the 404-for-a-truncated-link behaviour or the reporting. Capturing where the code has already decided "this is a real fault" keeps both and keeps mapped 404s and 403s out of the inbox.
- **2026-09-12** — **No source-map upload.** `withSentryConfig` needs `SENTRY_AUTH_TOKEN` at build time — a secret in CI and a step that fails a deploy when it expires. Worth adding when somebody is actually reading these traces.
- **2026-09-12** — **`/admin/health` is a Next route handler requiring a session, proxying the public API endpoint.** Browser → Next → API is the app's rule and there is no CORS to keep in step; requiring only sign-in rather than the platform flag avoids a second round trip per 60-second poll for something a signed-in person may know, and `/admin` redirects a non-operator regardless.
